# IRCTC Hotel Booking Website — UX Evaluation and Redesign

## 1. Project Overview

This project was carried out as part of CCA-III to evaluate the user experience of the IRCTC Hotel Booking Website and develop a redesigned prototype to improve usability, navigation, and the overall hotel-booking experience.

The project involved analysing the original website, conducting qualitative user testing through Think-Aloud sessions, collecting participant feedback, and creating a redesigned interface using Figma. The aim was to identify usability issues faced by users and propose a more intuitive, organised, and user-friendly design.

## 2. Project Objectives

- To evaluate the usability of the original IRCTC Hotel Booking Website.
- To understand users' difficulties, expectations, and frustrations while navigating the website.
- To conduct Think-Aloud testing sessions with five participants.
- To record and transcribe participants' verbal feedback.
- To identify usability issues and opportunities for improvement.
- To design a high-fidelity prototype that improves navigation, information presentation, hotel comparison, and the booking flow.
- To compare the original website experience with the redesigned prototype and draw conclusions based on user feedback.

The redesigned prototype focuses on making the hotel-booking journey clearer and easier to follow. It includes the following key screens and features:

- **Homepage:** A prominent hotel-search section and popular destinations.
- **Search Results:** Hotel listings with prices, ratings, amenities, and filtering options.

- **Booking Details and Checkout:** Guest information fields, payment-option layout, and a price breakdown.
- **Booking Journey:** A more structured flow from hotel selection to guest details and the proposed confirmation stage.

*Note: The prototype is for demonstration and usability evaluation. Sample rates and booking details are illustrative; the prototype does not make real hotel reservations or process payments.*

## 5. Methodology

The project was completed through the following stages:

### Step 1: Evaluation of the Original Website

We explored the original IRCTC Hotel Booking Website to understand its layout, navigation, hotel-search process, available filters, property details, and booking flow. This helped us establish the baseline user experience.

### Step 2: Think-Aloud Testing

We conducted one-on-one Think-Aloud sessions with five participants. Participants were asked to explore the original website and express their thoughts, expectations, difficulties, and frustrations while performing the assigned tasks.

The sessions helped us understand the experience from the users' perspectives rather than relying only on our own assumptions.

### Step 3: Audio Recording and Transcription

The Think-Aloud sessions were recorded, and the audio recordings were converted into text transcripts. These recordings and transcripts provide qualitative material for reviewing participant feedback and identifying usability issues.

### Step 4: Identification of Usability Issues

We reviewed the observations and feedback to identify areas where the user experience could be improved. The evaluation focused on aspects such as:

- Ease of navigation and locating hotel-booking options.
- Clarity of the hotel-search process.
- Readability and organisation of hotel information.
- Ease of comparing hotel prices, ratings, and facilities.
- Accessibility of room details and cancellation policies.
- Clarity of the checkout process and total booking cost.

### Step 5: Prototype Design in Figma

Based on the identified opportunities for improvement, we created a redesigned hotel-booking prototype in Figma. The design emphasises a clearer search interface, organised hotel listings, accessible property information, and a more structured booking flow.

### Step 6: Prototype Evaluation and Comparison

The redesigned prototype was considered alongside the original website to assess how the proposed design addresses the identified usability concerns. The comparison focused on layout clarity, information accessibility, hotel comparison, and the overall booking journey.

## 7. Key Outcomes

The project helped us understand the importance of evaluating a website through real user interactions. The key outcomes include:

- A structured evaluation of the original IRCTC Hotel Booking Website.
- Qualitative feedback gathered through Think-Aloud testing.
- Audio recordings and text transcripts of participant sessions.
- Identification of usability concerns and potential design improvements.
- A high-fidelity Figma prototype illustrating a redesigned hotel-booking experience.
- A comparison of the original website and the proposed redesign.

The redesigned interface aims to help users find relevant hotel information more easily, compare properties more efficiently, and understand the booking steps and cost breakdown more clearly.

## 8. Final Conclusion

CCA-III provided us with practical experience in the process of user experience evaluation and interface redesign. We began by exploring the original IRCTC Hotel Booking Website to understand its structure and the steps involved in finding and selecting a hotel.

We then conducted Think-Aloud sessions with five participants, allowing us to observe how users interacted with the website and listen to their expectations, difficulties, and suggestions. Recording and transcribing these sessions helped us organise the qualitative feedback and identify areas where the user experience could be improved.

Using these observations as a basis for our design work, we developed a redesigned prototype in Figma. The prototype presents a more structured hotel-search interface, organised hotel listings, detailed property information, and a clearer checkout layout with a price breakdown. These design choices aim to make the hotel-booking journey more intuitive and reduce the effort required to locate and compare relevant information.

Overall, this project helped us understand that effective UX design involves more than creating an attractive interface. It requires observing real users, understanding their needs, identifying usability problems, and translating those findings into practical design improvements. It also strengthened our skills in qualitative research, usability testing, information organisation, prototyping, teamwork, and documentation.

The redesigned prototype represents a proposed improvement rather than a fully validated replacement for the original website. Further usability testing and interaction checks would be necessary to confirm whether the changes consistently improve task completion, efficiency, and user satisfaction.
